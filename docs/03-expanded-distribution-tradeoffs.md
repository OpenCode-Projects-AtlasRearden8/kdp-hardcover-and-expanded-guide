# 3. Expanded distribution tradeoffs

Three checkboxes exist on KDP when you publish a paperback or hardcover:

- **KDP Select** — Kindle ebook exclusivity (not this guide; see *Publishing Beyond KDP Guide*).
- **Expanded distribution (ED)** for paperback and hardcover — reach outside Amazon's own retail sites.
- **Preorders** — covered in [chapter 4](04-preorders-without-surprises.md).

This chapter is about ED. It is the least glamorous button in KDP, costs nothing to press, and quietly changes your numbers the moment anyone outside Amazon buys your book.

## What expanded distribution actually does

Expanded distribution lists your paperback or hardcover with third-party wholesalers — the plumbing that independent bookstores, academic suppliers, and library systems use to order books. When a shop or library buys:

- They order from the wholesaler, not from you.
- KDP fulfills, prints, and ships it.
- **You earn less** — and how much less is the whole tradeoff.

ED is a *reach* tool, not a *growth* tool. You're not likely to see a blizzard of bookstore sales from it. The channels it opens — especially libraries — are the reason to press it.

## Important: ED is not "going wide"

Be careful with vocabulary, because it bites:

- **"Going wide"** means selling *your* ebooks and paperbooks directly on multiple retail platforms — Apple Books, Google Play, Barnes & Noble, Kobo, or via a distributor like Draft2Digital. That's a different strategic decision, covered in *Publishing Beyond KDP Guide*.
- **Expanded distribution** is a *single Amazon settings change* that makes your *print* book orderable through wholesalers outside Amazon. It doesn't touch your ebook at all.

You can do one, both, or neither. Most beginners do neither, then wonder why their book isn't in their local library's catalog. If the library question is real for you, ED is the right first tool.

## The money, in plain English

Normal KDP print royalty (on the Amazon marketplace itself):

> **Amazon sale:** (60% × list price) − printing cost

ED applies its own royalty rate, which is lower:

> **Expanded-distribution sale:** (a lower % × list price) − printing cost

The difference is a chunk of your margin, taken by the wholesaler chain that made the sale possible. Your **list price doesn't change**; your **take per sale does** — noticeably.

### Illustrative workbook

*Round, invented numbers for teaching. Verify against KDP's current royalty and printing-cost help pages for your real book.*

Same 300-page book, list **$26.99** hardcover (case laminate), printing cost ~$9.15:

| | Amazon marketplace sale | Expanded-distribution sale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Royalty rate applied | 60% | (a lower rate — verify current number) |
| Gross before print cost | $16.19 | (lower) |
| Printing cost | ~$9.15 | ~$9.15 |
| **You keep, per unit** | ~$7.04 | (noticeably less) |

The ED table is deliberately rough. The point: the printing cost is the same, the gross is smaller, so what you keep is noticeably less. We deliberately avoid pasting a percentage — KDP has shifted this rate across editions, and the wrong number stuck in your head is worse than the right method.

**How to read the numbers yourself (the durable method):**

1. Find your **list price** on KDP's pricing page.
2. Check KDP's current **royalty rate for expanded distribution** (help page: print royalties).
3. Subtract your **printing cost** exactly once.
4. Compare that ED per-unit take with the Amazon per-unit take.
5. Multiply the difference by **how many ED-channel sales you realistically expect.** For most beginner nonfiction: dozens, not thousands.

If the realistic total difference is a few hundred dollars a year, and the library visibility question matters to you at all, the checkbox is almost certainly worth it. If not, it's a coin toss and either answer is fine.

## Other tradeoffs, besides money

- **Price floor rises.** Expanded distribution carries list-price minimums (KDP publishes them). If you're priced tight, ED may not fit your floor at all — KDP will just say so on the pricing page.
- **Returns.** Bookstores and libraries expect the right to **return unsold copies** for credit. That behavior is part of the wholesale channel's cost of doing business; a returned copy is a sale that reverses. Its smallness is exactly why it barely dents a beginner's numbers.
- **Preorders are separate machinery.** If you set a KDP preorder, confirm what it covers — **do not blind-assume it applies to ED channels** — see [chapter 4](04-preorders-without-surprises.md).
- **You lose fine control of channel pricing.** Some channels mark up or set sale prices. The list price is yours; the street price isn't always.
- **Nothing, literally nothing, stops you doing it later.** ED can be turned on after publication once your files and price are settled. You don't have to decide today.

## When ED is worth it, in one line each

- **Your one-line library case:** readers who share your topic will find a hardcover through library systems; free shelf-space nowhere else sells it.
- **Your one-line gift-store case:** small gift shops near your niche (family caregiving, chess clubs, plant shops) order through wholesalers; you never have to make an awkward apology call.
- **Your one-line credibility case:** "ask your local library to order it" becomes a *real* sentence in a newsletter instead of a soft promise.

## When ED isn't worth it (yet)

- You priced your book *tight* (below ~$9.99 paperback / ~$19.99 hardcover for normal sizes) — the floor's math starts failing.
- You need every dollar of margin to fund a specific next project in a short window.
- Your book's audience is Amazon-native: they read on devices, order on Amazon, and never visit a library or an independent store.

## The calm procedure

1. **Settle both files, both editions, both prices** before touching ED. It can be enabled later without touching anything else.
2. Check the **price floor** on KDP's pricing page with the ED checkbox ticked. If the floor jumps above your target price, walk away calmly and re-enable later.
3. Tick the box, click publish, forget about it. Do not build a fantasy around B&N shelf placement; ED's help in year one is usually libraries.
4. Set a reminder to check KDP's royalty report in a few months, filtered by channel, and **sanity-check the ED numbers against your checklist** (see [checklists](checklists.md)).

> **Verify note.** KDP's exact ED royalty rate, price floors, return rules, and page-count limits are published on KDP's help pages and do change. Check the current print-book royalty page and expanded-distribution page before pressing the button.
