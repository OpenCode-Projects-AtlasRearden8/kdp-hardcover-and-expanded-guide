# 5. Author copies for events and giveaways

Author copies are **your own book, printed at cost, bought by you** — no royalty percolation, no marketplace commission. They are the best deal in KDP and the least discussed. This chapter covers ordering them, what they cost, and three honest uses: signing events, review mailouts, and giveaways.

## What author copies are

When you publish on KDP, you can order physical copies of your own books **at the printing cost** — not at the listing price, and not from a retail channel. KDP prints the books, sends them to you, and no royalty is calculated (you're not selling to yourself; you're buying inventory).

Facts to understand up front (verify specifics on KDP's author-copy help page — availability varies by marketplace):

- **Cost = printing cost.** The same per-unit number from the royalty formula, now working for you instead of against you.
- **Author copies for hardcovers are typically orderable in fewer marketplaces than paperbacks** — often the US marketplace only. If your hardcover targets events in other countries, budget for international shipping of US-printed copies.
- **Shipping is real.** Heavier books are heavier boxes. Factor it before promising anyone anything.
- **You can order in bulk** at print cost, in reasonable quantities, and pay with your marketplaces' billing methods — this is where "roughly 39–50% cheaper per unit than buying retail" comes from: you skip the retail markup *and* the royalty middleman both ways.

## Why author copies change your event math

Two tables compare, using the same illustrative numbers as earlier chapters — a 300-page book:

| One giveaway copy | Paperback | Hardcover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List price (what a reader pays) | $14.99 | $26.99 |
| Author copy cost (illustrative) | ~$4.45 | ~$9.15 |
| Royalty you would have earned on an Amazon sale | ~$4.54 | ~$7.04 |
| **Effective cost of one giveaway** (author copy + shipping) | **~$5–7** | **~$10–14** |

| One event-sale copy | Paperback | Hardcover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You pay (author copy, illustrative) | ~$4.45 + shipping | ~$9.15 + shipping |
| You sell at | $14.99 | $26.99 (or a fair "event price") |
| **You keep per unit** | ~$9–10 | ~$15–17 |

Both tables say the same thing at different volumes: **author copies are your wholesale channel, with you as the store.** A signing event is essentially you retailing your own inventory — which is why event books are one of the *rare* places margin is truly yours.

## Three honest uses

### 1. Signing events

- **Bring both formats if you have both.** The paperback is the "yes, I'll buy" choice; the hardcover is the "this is a real gift" choice. Two price points sell to two kinds of wallets without either feeling bad.
- **Order 10–20% more books than your head-count estimate.** Sold out is fine; one hour of "we're out" is fine; three hours is a wasted slot.
- **Carry a price sign, a cash box with change, and a card reader.** Most event buyers pay by card, and a $27 book with no way to take that $27 is a decision the buyer quietly doesn't make.
- **Sign and date with a pen that won't ghost** (bleed through). Check your interior paper darkness with a real test copy *before* pressing in a sharpie in front of a line of people.

### 2. Review and influencer mailouts

- **A physical, personalized copy** to a handful of carefully chosen reviewers (librarians of niche newsletters, topic podcast hosts) is more likely to get read than an ebook ARC, for physical-attention reasons that are just true.
- **Cap the list before you start.** Ten thoughtfully mailed copies beat fifty scattershot ones. Author copies plus shipping is real budget — count it, set it, stop at it.
- Include a **one-page postcard** (index card weight, not letterhead): who this is for, in one sentence, and how to reach you.

### 3. Giveaways

- **Giveaway where the fish are.** Your topic's niche community — a caregiving forum, chess club raffle, FFXIV free company — is worth more per copy than a scattershot Amazon "win a gift card" blast where entrants mostly aren't readers.
- **Print a giveaway budget** and treat it like the LLC budget line it is (see *LLC First Year Handbook* for the bookkeeping side). A physical book + shipping given to one stranger costs about $10–15 hardcover; fifty of them is a real purchase, not a splurge.
- **Ask for the win at the end:** "If the book helps, a one-line review at the place you got it does more for me than the book sale." Successful giveaways feed the next sale.

## Ordering author copies, calmly

1. Confirm your **files are truly final** — author copies print the live file, and reprints aren't free.
2. Open KDP's **author copies page** for the marketplace you're eligible to order in.
3. Choose quantity and shipping; note the **per-unit + shipping total** per copy.
4. **Order one test copy first** for any new format or finish (particularly a new hardcover finish), *then* the bulk order. A book you sell to a stranger should look like the listing photo.
5. Put the invoice in your **bookkeeping folder** (see *Quarterly Taxes Calm Guide* for how this is an inventory expense).

## When author copies are the wrong tool

- **You want readers to buy from Amazon.** Amazon sales boost your marketplace rank and feed the recommendation engine. Mass-mailing free copies undercuts that; keep giveaways small and targeted.
- **You're trying to hit an international event on a printing-cost budget**, but your KDP hardcover author copies only ship from the US — international freight usually eats the advantage. Look into a local print-on-demand printer for events abroad before you order 200 units across an ocean.
- **Marketing on paper beats marketing on pixels for your topic?** If your community lives online and buys on Kindle, author copies are a boutique tool, not the engine.

> **Verify note.** Author-copy availability by marketplace, bulk-order limits, shipping pricing, and what counts as an inventory expense for your specific situation are all things to double-check — the first set on KDP's help site, the last with a qualified tax professional.
