# 6. Royalty differences in plain English

Everything in this guide eventually lands in one question: **"how much do I actually keep?"** This chapter answers it once, side by side, in plain English — ebook, paperback, both hardcover finishes, Amazon sales, and expanded-distribution sales.

## The only two formulas you need

KDP print and ebook royalties are simple arithmetic. No tricks are hiding in the fine print that matter at beginner scale.

**Ebooks (Kindle):**

> **35% tier:** you keep **35%** of list.
> **70% tier:** you keep **70%** of list, minus a small per-megabyte delivery fee, and only at supported price points.

**Print (paperback and hardcover, both finishes):**

> **You keep 60% of list payment minus the printing cost** for sales on Amazon's marketplaces (verify the current rate — KDP publishes it).
> **Expanded-distribution sales use a lower royalty rate**, minus the same printing cost (see [chapter 3](03-expanded-distribution-tradeoffs.md)).

Note what's missing: **hardcovers have no 70% option.** The 35/70 choice is an *ebook-only* choice. Print is always one rate for Amazon sales, one lower rate for ED sales, priced against a printing cost that varies by format, page count, and finish.

## The one comparison table

*All figures illustrative. Same 300-page book. Real numbers come from KDP's pricing and cost-calculator pages for your edition — verify there.*

| Channel | List | Royalty window | Reduced by | Illustrative take | % of list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kindle ebook, 70% tier | $9.99 | 70% | ~$0.15 delivery | ~$6.84 | ~68% |
| Kindle ebook, 35% tier | $9.99 | 35% | — | ~$3.50 | 35% |
| Paperback, Amazon | $14.99 | 60% | ~$4.45 print | ~$4.54 | ~30% |
| Paperback, expanded dist. | $14.99 | lower rate | ~$4.45 print | less | smallest |
| Hardcover (case laminate), Amazon | $26.99 | 60% | ~$9.15 print | ~$7.04 | ~26% |
| Hardcover (jacket), Amazon | $26.99 | 60% | ~$10.60 print | ~$5.59 | ~21% |
| Hardcover, expanded dist. | $26.99 | lower rate | ~$9.15 print | less | smallest |

Three things this table should teach at a glance:

1. **The ebook is your high-percentage channel.** Library-visibility aside, nothing else comes close to the 70% rate.
2. **Print royalties collapse as a percentage** as printing cost grows. Hardcovers *can* earn more *dollars* per unit while earning a *smaller share* of list — both facts are true at once (see [chapter 1](01-when-a-hardcover-earns-its-setup-cost.md)).
3. **Expanded distribution takes the smallest cut of any channel in the same edition.** That's the price of reaching stores you can't reach yourself. If your book's only realistic buyers are on Amazon, ticking that checkbox is visiting a room with no chairs.

## A worked walkthrough (illustrative)

Say you sell **100 copies of this book in three channels**: 60 ebooks, 30 paperbacks, 10 hardcovers.

| Channel | Units | Take per unit (illustrative) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kindle ebook (70%) | 60 | ~$6.84 | ~$410 |
| Paperback (Amazon) | 30 | ~$4.54 | ~$136 |
| Hardcover case laminate (Amazon) | 10 | ~$7.04 | ~$70 |
| **Total to you** | | | **~$616** |

Now the same units, but priced lazily — hardcover at $19.99 (anything to be "cheaper than expected"):

| | List | 60% | Print | Take per unit | Units | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hardcover, lazily priced | $19.99 | $11.99 | ~$9.15 | ~$2.84 | 10 | ~$28 |

**Pricing the hardcover $7 lower leaves ~$42 on the table for ten sales** — and that's a *pattern*, not a one-off: pricing-to-be-liked is genuinely expensive on a high-cost-format book. Price the hardcover where its real cost is; readers who want it cheaper own Kindles and won't visit its page anyway.

## Timing: when the money lands

KDP pays **about 60 days after the end of the month in which the sale occurred** (verify the current schedule), and only after you clear a small minimum threshold and have your tax information on file. First-royalty-cycle beginners routinely describe this as the most confusing part of KDP — it isn't a mistake, it's just the calendar.

- Keep a simple **royalty ledger** in a spreadsheet: date, units, royalty, notes. (Our *KDP Sales Tracker Webapp* does this; a plain spreadsheet works fine too.)
- Expect the **preorder revenue reporting** to show up around release week; treat any pre-release preview as a preview, not a payout.
- If you're an LLC, **be consistent in your tax info** — the payee and the entity should match across KDP's system. See *LLC First Year Handbook* for entity admin.

## Differences that beginners find surprising anyway

- **Delivery fee affects ebooks only**; the hardcover's equivalent bite is the *printing cost*, and it's bigger.
- **Minutes-read and page reads** (KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited) pay per page read, not per unit sold — and a 700-page dense reference book page-reads differently from a 90-page skim. (That whole topic lives in the sibling Series and Boxset guide and *Publishing Beyond KDP Guide*.)
- **Returns in ED** reverse a royalty when a store sends unsold copies back. Small at beginner scale, but it's why your monthly number can wobble slightly.
- **Different marketplaces pay in different currencies**; if you enable several, you'll see line items per marketplace in your report.

The consensus of all of these: **Royalty differences aren't moral questions — they're plumbing.** Choose your channels on purpose, price for each format's real cost, record everything, and the plumbing works.

> **Verify note.** The flat "60% of list payment minus printing cost" is *the thing to verify most carefully* in this guide: KDP's current print royalty help page is the authority, and this is exactly the kind of number that has changed before and will change again.
