# KDP Hardcover and Expanded Guide

A calm, plain-English manual for KDP's *harder* buttons: hardcover releases, case laminate vs. dust jacket, expanded distribution, preorders, author copies, and the royalty differences between all of them.

**By Asdale Dynamics, LLC**

---

## Who this guide is for

You already published a paperback (or ebook) on Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP), it went fine, and now you're staring at buttons you've never pressed:

- **Hardcover.** It costs nothing to *set up*, but it quietly changes your math — different royalty rate window, different printing cost, different file requirements.
- **Case laminate vs. dust jacket.** Two hardcover finishes with two different cover files and two different cost stories.
- **Expanded distribution.** The checkbox that puts your book in bookstores and libraries — in exchange for a lower royalty and stricter rules.
- **Preorders.** The "reserve a copy early" tool, which is great until it surprises you.
- **Author copies.** Real printed copies at print cost, for signing events, review mailouts, and giveaways.

If you've never published with KDP at all, start with the sibling guide, **First KDP Book Roadmap Guide** — it covers your first launch end to end. This guide assumes you already know what a trim size and a cover PDF are.

This guide is grounded in the real experience of publishing *The Massive AI Manual* — a book from [Asdale Dynamics, LLC](https://dylanasdale.github.io) that explains AI "like a friend would" — now in hardcover alongside its paperback and Kindle editions. The chapters below come from actually pressing these buttons, once, carefully, and writing down what we wish we'd known.

## The short version, up front

If you read nothing else, here's the honest summary this guide expands on:

1. **A hardcover is a tool, not a trophy.** It earns its place when you need archival-quality copies for libraries, gift buyers, or signing events — not because it makes your product page look fuller. The unit economics are worse than paperback almost everywhere.
2. **Expanded distribution is a library-and-bookstore checkbox, not a sales strategy.** It trades a chunk of royalty for reach into channels you can't easily sell into yourself. For most beginner nonfiction books, the honest case is *can't-reach-elsewhere* places like libraries; comparison is easy: the lower rate applies only to the channels ED feeds. (Chapter 3 is a full tradeoff table.)
3. **Preorders reward finished books.** Set a preorder price you're sure about, upload final files before enabling the button, and never use preorder dates you can't hit — Amazon penalizes cancellations and "missing the date" hurts more than not preordering at all.
4. **Author copies are your best deal in KDP.** Print cost, no royalty middleman, roughly 39–50% cheaper per copy than buying the retail copy you sell. That's your event margin.

## How to use this guide

Read chapters 1–2 before you build a single hardcover file. Chapters 3–6 can be read in order or consulted when you reach each decision. Chapter 7 is a flowchart you can walk through hands-on. The [checklists](checklists.md) page condenses everything.

## Important disclaimers

- **Education, not advice.** This guide explains KDP concepts and processes in plain English. It is not legal, tax, or accounting advice. For decisions with real money or legal consequences, talk to a qualified professional.
- **Numbers age.** Royalty rates, printing-cost figures, page-count minimums, and channel availability change. Every number in this guide is either marked *illustrative* or paired with a **verify against current KDP help documentation** note. The *method* in each chapter is durable; the *specifics* are dated.
- **No affiliate links, no upsells.** This guide earns nothing when you sign up for anything, KDP or otherwise.
