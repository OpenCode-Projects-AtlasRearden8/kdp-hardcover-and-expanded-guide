2. Case laminate vs. jacket math

KDP hardcovers come in two finishes, and the choice is genuinely a cost decision, not just an aesthetic one. This chapter lays them side by side, then walks through the math that decides — using clearly marked illustrative numbers.

The two finishes, in one breath

  • Case laminate (KDP calls it hardcover without jacket): the cover art is printed directly onto the hard case. No dust jacket. Looks like a library children's book or most modern photography books.
  • Hardcover with dust jacket (case + jacket): a printed or cloth-colored hard case underneath, plus a removable dust jacket — the glossy "paper band" with flaps you see on most adult hardbacks in a store.

Everything else — royalty rate, distribution options, the "no 70% tier" rule — is identical between them. What differs is: files you must supply, per-unit print cost, and who the format says you're for.

Side by side

Spine width depends on Book's page count Book's page count (same — the case thickness matches)

Shows off in photos Sharp, art-forward front cover Photogenic, with flap copy for blurbs
Best fit Reference books, children's books, photo books, desk manuals Narrative nonfiction, memoir, "gift-ceremony" books, things you sign

Take these as directional — the real per-unit difference depends on your page count and trim, so run both through KDP's printing-cost calculator for your actual book before deciding.

The math, in plain English

The royalty formula is the same for both finishes:

Royalty = (60% × list price) − printing cost

The whole case-laminate-vs-jacket decision lives inside two variables: the list price you choose and the printing cost you're charged.

Illustrative worked example

Round, invented numbers for teaching. Your real numbers come from KDP's cost calculator for your trim and page count — verify there before pricing.

Suppose a 300-page, 6 × 9″ nonfiction book priced at $26.99 in both finishes:

Case laminate With dust jacket
60% of list $16.19 $16.19
Printing cost (illustrative) ~$9.15 ~$10.60
You keep, per unit, on Amazon ~$7.04 ~$5.59
Difference per unit — you give up ~$1.45 per sale
Difference per 100 sales — ~$145

The jacket costs you roughly $1.50 a copy at this size — because it's an extra printed sheet, folded, with flaps. On a $27 book, a $1.50 haircut is honest but meaningful: if you're pricing tight (say, $19.99 for a slim technical book), the jacket can be the difference between a workable margin and a losing one.

The pricing rule that falls out of this

Behind KDP, each format has a minimum list price that covers the printing cost plus a minimum royalty. Hardcover minimums are higher than paperback minimums for the same page count, and jacket minimums are a bit above case laminate minimums. Before you fall in love with a finish:

  1. Run your page count and trim through KDP's printing cost calculator for both finishes.
  2. Read the resulting minimum list price for each.
  3. Ask: can I sell at or comfortably above that minimum without embarrassing myself?

If the jacket's minimum pushes your price past what your audience will pay, case laminate is your answer — done.

How to choose in five questions

  1. Do readers remove dust jackets when reading on a desk? For job-aids and reference use, probably yes — and a removed jacket is a lost billboard. Choose case laminate.
  2. Is the book a gift or a ceremony object? Weddings, retirements, memorials, graduations, milestone birthdays. Jacket carries the gift-weight people expect.
  3. Will you sign them at events? You can sign either — but jackets have a nice signing surface on the inner flap, and a jacket photographs better on social media. Hand this question to your marketing brain instead of your accounting one.
  4. Is your margin thin? Jackets cost roughly $1.50 more per unit in our illustrative math. On a slim book, that can be fatal. Run your own numbers.
  5. Do you want the reader to think "durable, keep forever"? Case laminate communicates that instantly. Jacket communicates "important, handle with care." Choose the signal that matches how the book should get used.

What the files look like

  • Case laminate: one PDF, full cover spread — front + spine + back — plus a bleed so the case art prints past the edge exactly like a paperback cover file. Spine width comes from KDP's cover calculator, based on page count.
  • With dust jacket: two PDFs. The jacket file has the usual front/spine/back plus fold-over flaps on each end — KDP's template sizes the flaps for you. The case underneath can be a simple solid color (us publishing tradition: cloth-look or a plain accent). Many publishers use a plain dark case and let the jacket do all the talking.

Verify note. Exact file specs, flap sizes, and spine-width rules come from KDP's cover templates — download the current template for your trim before you design. Templates change; screenshots in old tutorials are often wrong.

Our actual choice, and why

For The Massive AI Manual (a beginner's AI desk reference, well over 300 pages) we chose case laminate. Three reasons, in order:

  1. The use case is "keep it on your desk next to your keyboard" — a dust jacket would be off the book by day two and lost by week two.
  2. The jacket's lower margin was real money on every sale.
  3. Libraries — our main third-party hardcover target through expanded distribution — habitually discard dust jackets anyway, so the jacket buys us nothing there.

If your book is closer to memoir or a gift book, our answer doesn't transfer. Yours will be different, and that's the point of the five questions above.
